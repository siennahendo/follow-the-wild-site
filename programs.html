Focus area 1 / Emerging Storytellers Scholarship & Mentorship

A story to tell.
A little help to tell it.

Scholarships, mentorship and creative resources help aspiring wildlife photographers, filmmakers and creators develop their stories and share them. A small award can make a real difference. So can time with someone who knows how to turn an idea into a compelling video, photo story or live conversation.

We’re designing a small first cohort, pairing project support with experienced mentors. We’re especially interested in storytellers with local knowledge, a distinctive perspective and a clear subject they want people to understand.

The scholarship program is in development. Applications are not open. Award amounts, eligibility, selection and timing will be announced when confirmed.

What a scholarship includes

Support for a specific story

Help with the costs of making the work: fieldwork, production, editing or access to the right equipment.

Time with a mentor

Practical guidance from an experienced storyteller on the idea, the craft and making work for its audience.

Creative resources

Access to equipment, editing help and a clear plan to publish and share the finished work.

Storytelling partnerships

We’re also exploring a program that pairs emerging creators with scientists, researchers and people working in wildlife and wild places who could benefit from storytelling support.

Creators help document and share that work, gaining hands-on experience while bringing important research and projects to a wider audience.

Focus area 2 / Wildlife Education & Outreach

Free to learn.
Open to young people.

We create free, accessible educational content about wildlife and the natural world.

We plan to partner with local schools, educators and youth organizations to bring these stories to younger audiences, in classrooms and in the places they already watch.

Schools and educators: get in touch ↗

Free educational content

Wildlife and nature stories anyone can watch, read and use, with no paywall.

School and educator partners

Working with local schools and teachers to bring these stories into classrooms.

Youth organizations

Reaching young people through the groups and programs they already belong to.

How we’ll report our progress

Show the work.
Share what we learn.

As projects launch, we plan to report scholarships awarded, mentoring delivered, educational resources created and work published. We’ll also share available audience measures such as views, watch time and engagement.

Those figures tell us how stories are being seen. Where a project includes a school session or another direct activity, we’ll report participation and feedback separately. Learning and changes in behavior need their own evidence.
